Local Observability Cockpit

Letterbot includes a built-in web dashboard that runs entirely on your machine. No cloud login, no subscription, no telemetry. Just open a browser.

Access the cockpit: while Letterbot is running, open http://127.0.0.1:8787 in any browser on the same machine. Read-only — nothing in the cockpit modifies email or config.
📊

Dashboard

System status overview: IMAP connection health per account, Telegram bot status, processing queue, last sync time, active mode (FULL / DEGRADED).

📧

Email archive

History of all processed emails: type, priority, reason code, detected entities, delivery status. Filter by account, date, priority, or email type.

🔍

AI decision trace

For each email: why was this priority assigned? Which signals contributed? What did the LLM return (if enabled)? Full evidence chain, no black box.

💚

System health

Per-component health: IMAP accounts, Telegram, LLM provider, database. With specific error messages when something is wrong.

What SLA metrics does the cockpit show?
Delivery rate (% of processed emails successfully sent to Telegram), error rate, p90 latency (90th percentile end-to-end processing time from email arrival to Telegram delivery), and salvage rate (% of initially-failed deliveries that succeeded on retry).
What is the AI decision trace?
For every prioritized email, the decision trace records: the final priority and reason codes, the individual signals that contributed (sender score, email type, LLM output if used), the correction history from Telegram buttons, and the email's current snooze state. This makes the system auditable at any time.
Does the cockpit connect to the internet?
No. The cockpit is a local Flask/Uvicorn server bound to 127.0.0.1:8787 only. It reads from the local SQLite database. No outbound network calls. It is not accessible from other machines on the network without explicit configuration.
Does the cockpit show email content?
The archive shows metadata — sender, subject, type, priority, reason codes, entities. Full email bodies and attachments are not stored in the database and therefore not shown. This is by design: the cockpit is safe to leave open without exposing raw correspondence.
